# Where the data comes from

On the sample project the app opens on, every document and record the swarm reads is written by [swarm/planning_case.py](swarm/planning_case.py), [swarm/site_case.py](swarm/site_case.py),
[swarm/generator.py](swarm/generator.py) and [swarm/project.py](swarm/project.py). No file, table or figure from a real project, company, person or dataset is
in it, and no real data was used to fit or calibrate it.

The one real source is public: the workforce and material norms in [swarm/norms.py](swarm/norms.py) are copied from the Indonesian national standard **SNI 7394:2008**,
*Tata cara perhitungan harga satuan pekerjaan beton untuk konstruksi bangunan gedung dan perumahan*, and were checked against its tables. Its section 6.10 prints the units
of sand and gravel as m3 beside figures that are kg in every other grade of the table; they are treated as kg.

## What is not synthetic

The table above describes the sample project. Three things bring in data that is not synthetic, and only if a person does it:

- **A project you create** holds what you enter, in its own file under `data/firm/projects/`.
- **A document or photo you upload** is yours, is kept under `data/firm/uploads/`, and is marked as uploaded. It stays on this machine until you press a button that asks a real model to read it. **Plan it with Muse** sends the Meta Model API the pages the agents need, from the documents not set aside. Bid and award records, instructions to bidders and empty notes are set aside by rule and never sent, unless a person overrules that file by file.

- **The regulation register** in [swarm/regulations.py](swarm/regulations.py) quotes public Indonesian regulations from their official texts on JDIH BPK, peraturan.go.id and the DPR's compilation of UU 2/2017, and records which are still in force, as checked on 2026-11-03.

- **What the agents remember** holds what people on a project decide and write, in its own folder beside that project's records (`data/firm/memory/`, or `data/firm/projects/memory/` for a project you create). It stays on this machine unless an agent is asked on a real model, which sends what that agent remembers along with its records.

The tender documents in [docs/](docs/README.md) are real and public, and they name real officials and companies. The app does not read them unless someone uploads them. The tests in [tests/test_package.py](tests/test_package.py) read the Unair and UGM packages on this machine, with a scripted model. Those two have been sent to the Meta Model API in full runs of the analysis, less the files set aside as not for the site. The two added on 2026-11-04, from UNP and KKP, have not. The rules that sort a package and read its tables were written from the first two, so how well they do on those says little about a third, and [the newer two already show it](docs/README.md#how-the-app-handles-them-on-2026-11-04).

## Assumptions we chose

These are design choices, not measured values:

| Assumption | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Differences that count as agreeing | 5 percentage points | `TOLERANCE_PCT` in `swarm/reconcile.py` |
| Evidence too old to show the current state | 21 days before the cut-off | `STALE_DAYS` in `swarm/config.py` |
| How close a lesson must be to reach a recommendation | 0.12, chosen on hand-written cases | `FLOOR` in `swarm/memory.py` |
| How close a model's words must be to count as repeating a decided recommendation | 0.6, chosen on hand-written cases | `SAME_WORDS` in `swarm/module_agents.py` |
| Control budget against the BoQ's sell value | 88% | `COST_SHARE` in `swarm/project.py` |
| Share of each budget already committed | 97% | `COMMIT_SHARE` in `swarm/project.py` |
| Actual cost against earned value, by package | 0.97 to 1.08 | `cost_factor` in `PACKAGES`, `swarm/project.py` |
| Retention | 5% | `RETENTION` in `swarm/project.py` |
| Share of verified work already certified, and share claimed this month | 78%, and 18% (34% for the one subcontractor set to bill ahead) | The seeding in `swarm/project.py` |
| Crew the pour needs | 26 | `CREW_NEEDED` in `swarm/project.py` |
| Effective hours in a person-day (OH) | 7 | `HOURS_PER_OH` in `swarm/norms.py` |
| Concrete spillage at the mixer, skip and tremie | 2.5% | `CONCRETE_WASTE_PCT` in `swarm/norms.py` |
| Plywood waste | 4.2%: the standard's 0.35 sheet per m2 against what a 1.22 x 2.44 m sheet covers | `PLYWOOD_NET_PER_M2` in `swarm/norms.py` |
| A quantity that agrees with the BoQ | Within 3% | `TOLERANCE` in `swarm/wbs.py` |
| Liquidated damages | Rp 25 million a day | `LD_PER_DAY` in `swarm/site_case.py` |
| A new project's starter breakdown, and 5 days for each of its activities | Invented, to be edited | `TEMPLATE` in `swarm/project.py` |
| Which scenario documents bear on which activities | A reading of the case | `DOC_LINKS` in `swarm/modules.py` |
| What a work package is worth, on a real package | Its group's value in the priced recap, spread by the master schedule's weights | `structure` in `swarm/package.py` |
| Smallest schedule row an agent may split | 5% of the contract | `SPLIT_FROM` in `swarm/package_firm.py` |
| When linked work runs side by side, not one after the other | Overlapping by more than half of the first | `link` in `swarm/package_firm.py` |
| Most pages one planner reads | 40 pages, 60,000 characters | `MOST_PAGES`, `MOST_TEXT` in `swarm/package_firm.py` |
| Working hours in a day, for a task's man-hours | 7 | `HOURS_A_DAY` in `swarm/package_firm.py` |
| The words that mark a task as special work under Permen PUPR 10/2021 Pasal 26 ayat (2) | Indonesian and English words for each of its ten kinds | `SPECIAL` in `swarm/regulations.py` |
| Which of a task's words count towards a work permit | Its name, steps and equipment. Its safety notes only for sparks, a fall or a scaffold on a task that does the work, and for live electrics, a confined space, diving or radiography on any. A check counts only if it is itself a live test, radiography or in a confined space. A task that gets the permit is not flagged. Nobody on a site has reviewed this | `permit_for` in `swarm/regulations.py` |
| What counts as lifting equipment for the safety risk level | Crane, tower crane, mobile crane, crawler crane, truck crane, pesawat angkat, passenger hoist | `LIFTING` in `swarm/regulations.py` |
| The owner's estimate for the safety risk level, where no table gives it | A figure after "senilai" in an estimate document. Where it lists several packages, the one named most like this project, and none if that does not settle it | `said_value` in `swarm/regulations.py` |
| Two documents disagree on a period | Different numbers of days, or working days against calendar days | `_disagree` in `swarm/regulations.py` |
| Floor to floor in the 3D timeline, where the drawings do not say | 4 m, and 3.5 m for a basement | `STOREY`, `BASEMENT` in `swarm/model3d.py` |
| The footprint where the drawings give no grid | A 4:3 box of the typical floor area, or else 30 by 18 m on 6 m bays | `DEFAULT` in `swarm/model3d.py` |
| What each work package builds, in the 3D timeline | Indonesian and English words for each kind of work, in its name and its control account's | `KINDS` in `swarm/model3d.py` |
| Work over every floor goes up floor by floor | Bottom up, each floor only once its structure is finished | `spread` and `after` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| Whether a project's piles are bored or driven, in the 3D timeline | Words for each, in the foundation packages' names and then the documents, whichever comes up more. Neither named: it just says piling | `BORED`, `DRIVEN` in `swarm/model3d.py` |
| The steps inside a floor's structure, in the 3D timeline | The slab takes the first 55% of its package and the columns the rest. On each bay: props and formwork, rebar from 30%, concrete from 60%. Props stay 14 days after the pour, and concrete shows as fresh for 21 days. Usual practice, not read from the documents | `buildScene` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| The steps inside other work, in the 3D timeline | Piles to 75% then caps and tie beams; bricks to 60% then plaster; frames to 50% then glass; trusses to 45% then tiles; cladding to 45% then fins. The scaffold comes down over the last 20% of the facade. Usual practice, not read from the documents | `buildScene` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| The basement pit, in the 3D timeline | Its floor 1.3 m under the lowest slab, 1.5 m of working space round the building, its sides cut back 0.8 m per metre of depth. Assumed | `footing`, `edgeX`, `edgeZ` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| The machines and crews in the 3D timeline | Excavator, dump truck, piling rig, concrete pump, mixer truck and the size of each crew are illustrative. How the crane climbs or comes and goes, and where it stands, are assumed | `buildScene` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| Whether the 3D timeline draws a lift | A lift or elevator named in a work package, then in the BoQ, specification, contract, drawings or scope. "Lubang lift" in a safety clause does not count | `LIFT`, `lift_source` in `swarm/model3d.py` |
| Where the stairs and the lift stand, in the 3D timeline | Two dog-leg stairs 2.8 m wide, one at each end by the long sides, and a 2.4 m lift shaft beside the first. Each floor's flights are cast with its columns unless the plan names its stairs. The stairwell is left open to the outside with a guard rail, as a tropical stairwell often is. Assumed: the drawings read do not show them | `STAIR_W`, `stairs`, `liftX` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| How one "Arsitektur" package is drawn in the 3D timeline | Walls over the first 55% of its dates, windows and doors from 40% to 80%, finishes from 50% to the end. Assumed: the package gives no detail | `phased` in `Model3DScene.ts` |
| Which crane the 3D timeline draws | The tower or mobile crane named in the work packages, then the BoQ, then the other documents. Wording that allows either names neither, and no crane is drawn where nothing says which | `TOWER`, `MOBILE`, `crane_kind` in `swarm/model3d.py` |

## How the photo counts were checked

Image models do not reliably draw an exact count, so every accepted photo was counted by eye (by Claude, during the build), and
`swarm/scenes.py` records what the image really shows, not what was asked for. Photos that broke a test scenario were generated
again. A view is treated as representative of its whole BoQ item.

## What the test score means

The score compares a run with the answer planted in the case. It only describes this synthetic project, and it stops being given once the
records change. It says nothing about how the swarm would do on a real project, and a score from the offline mock says nothing about accuracy
at all.
